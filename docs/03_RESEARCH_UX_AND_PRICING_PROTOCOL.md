# Research, UX, Assets, and Pricing Protocol

## Research output, not inspiration scraping

Create a dated, cited decision system. Do not browse mood boards and then imitate the prettiest page. Separate commercial competition from design benchmarking.

## Per-site evidence matrix

Record:

- URL, title, retrieval date, market, category;
- actual buyer and job-to-be-done;
- first-screen promise and proof proximity;
- offers and whether pricing is public;
- primary/secondary CTA destinations and steps to completion;
- form fields, progress, errors, mobile keyboard behavior, confirmation and recovery;
- case-study proof quality and whether outcomes are verifiable;
- navigation depth, information scent, readability, accessibility, performance observations;
- structured data, indexable page types, internal linking and content cadence;
- what is strong, weak, overused, confusing, or unavailable;
- an IronWake opportunity that can be delivered truthfully;
- screenshot/quote limits and IP notes.

Never copy a complete layout, distinctive animation, copy line, artwork, or proprietary interaction. Extract principles and create an original system.

## UX psychology requirements

Use ethical decision support:

1. Match the visitor's problem language before technology language.
2. Offer one primary next step per intent, with clear secondary escape paths.
3. Reduce uncertainty using scope, process, limitations, provider costs, and proof status near the CTA.
4. Progressive disclosure: show the smallest decision first, detail on request.
5. Use defaults only when they are genuinely the most common/safest choice; state why.
6. Preserve autonomy: no preselected paid upgrades, hidden fees, forced urgency, disguised ads, or guilt copy.
7. Make form effort visible and save progress only with consent/security.
8. Confirm the exact next state: received, awaiting confirmation, booked, paid, or failed.
9. Provide recovery without making the visitor restart.
10. Measure real completion, qualified lead rate, show rate, response time, and drop-off after launch.

## Guided offer selection

The customer should not face a wall of packages. Ask at most one screen at a time:

1. What outcome is failing? missed inquiries / booking / follow-up / reception / trust.
2. Where do inquiries arrive? phone / WhatsApp / website / social / multiple.
3. Rough monthly inquiry band.
4. Urgency and whether a current system exists.
5. Need: diagnostic, fix, managed system, or pilot.

Return:

- one recommended smallest-sufficient offer;
- why it fits;
- what it includes and explicitly excludes;
- setup range or “scope required” based on the approved ledger;
- separate recurring provider-cost estimate with source date;
- lower-cost and higher-capability alternatives;
- CTA to request an audit/scope, never a fake instant quote.

## India versus international

Do not attempt to prevent people from discovering another region's price. That is fragile and damages trust.

Use either `/in/pricing` and `/global/pricing`, or an explicit market selector with persistent URL state. Search engines receive correct canonicals/hreflang only when content truly targets different regions. Users can switch manually.

Differences must be explainable through:

- included discovery and customization;
- support hours/time-zone coverage;
- currency/tax/invoicing/jurisdiction;
- language/localization;
- provider/telephony costs;
- integration/compliance complexity;
- delivery and ongoing support level.

If the underlying scope is identical, do not hide arbitrary regional price discrimination. Use one base price with currency/tax explanation or offer localized service tiers with genuine scope differences.

## Asset truth

Every production asset requires:

```text
asset_id, path, role, source, creator, license/ownership, permission evidence,
allowed channels, expiry/attribution, alt-text decision, optimization variants
```

Generated abstract 2D/3D system visuals may be used if owned and not presented as documentary proof. Do not use AI-generated people/offices/client scenes as evidence. Do not hotlink Stitch's Google-hosted images.
